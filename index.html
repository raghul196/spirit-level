How to Use This Online Spirit Level

This online spirit level is a web-based application designed to check if a surface is perfectly level (horizontal) or plumb (vertical), using the sensors already built into your smartphone or tablet. It transforms your device into a powerful digital bubble level for free, without requiring any software installation. Everything runs directly in your web browser, making it easy to check alignment anywhere.

Core Functionality

  • Real-time Angle Measurement: Once you grant sensor access and press Start, the application reads data from your device's accelerometer and gyroscope to show the surface angle in real-time.
  • Surface and Wall Modes: Switch between 'Surface Mode' for checking horizontal surfaces (like tables) and 'Wall Mode' for vertical ones (like picture frames).
  • High-Precision Bubble Level: The interface features a virtual bubble that moves based on the device's tilt, providing an intuitive visual guide to achieving a perfect level.
  • Zero Calibration: The Zero button allows you to set any surface as a reference point (0 degrees). This is useful for measuring the angle of one surface relative to another. It can also be used to adjust for bumps on your mobile device like protruding cameras.
  • Audio Feedback: Activate audio feedback, and your device will beep when a perfect level is achieved, allowing you to level surfaces without constantly monitoring the screen.

Disclaimer: Understanding Accuracy

Please be aware that while this tool offers a convenient way to check levels, the sensors in a typical smartphone are not designed for metrology-grade measurement. Factors like sensor quality, device calibration, and even small bumps on your phone's case can introduce errors. For many DIY and hobbyist tasks, the accuracy is more than sufficient. However, for professional applications requiring certified precision, a dedicated, calibrated spirit level is recommended.

How to Improve Measurement Accuracy

You can significantly improve the accuracy of your measurement and compensate for sensor bias by using a simple two-step calibration method. This process effectively cancels out most of the phone's inherent zero-offset error.

  1. First Measurement: Place your phone on the surface you want to measure and note the angle reading (e.g. 0.8°).
  2. Second Measurement: Without lifting it, rotate your phone exactly 180° in the same spot and take a second reading (e.g. -1.0°).
  3. Calculate the True Offset: The true angle is the average of these two measurements. For example: (0.8 + (-1.0)) / 2 = -0.1°. This shows your surface is very close to level, with a small offset. A perfectly calibrated level would read 0.5° and -0.5° in this example if the surface had a 0.5° tilt.

Use the 180° rotation method to cancel out sensor errors and achieve a more accurate measurement.

How to Perform "Zero" Calibration

Zero calibration allows you to set your current surface as the new reference point (0 degrees), which is essential for measuring relative angles or compensating for uneven surfaces. This is how you do it:

  1. Place Device: Lay your smartphone or tablet firmly on the surface you wish to designate as your 0° reference.
  2. Stabilize: Ensure your device is stable and not moving.
  3. Press Zero to calibrate: Tap the Zero button on the spirit level interface.
  4. New Baseline: The current readings will instantly reset to 0°, establishing this orientation as your new baseline for all subsequent measurements.

Zero calibration sets the current surface as the 0° reference, allowing for relative angle measurements.

Adjusting for Protruding Cameras

Modern smartphones often have camera bumps that prevent them from lying perfectly flat. This can interfere with accurate measurements, but you can easily correct for it using the Zero button, as illustrated below.

  1. Place and Observe: Position your phone on the surface. Notice that the camera bump creates a slight, stable tilt, showing a non-zero reading.
  2. Press Zero: Tap the Zero button. This instructs the spirit level to treat the phone's current tilted position as the new "0°" reference point.
  3. Measure Accurately: The display will now read 0°. The effect of the camera bump is cancelled out, allowing you to take accurate measurements relative to the surface.

Use "Zero" to compensate for the tilt caused by a camera bump.
